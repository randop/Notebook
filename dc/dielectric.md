# Dielectric cooling

**Dielectric cooling (also known as immersion cooling)** — where servers are submerged in non-conductive dielectric fluids — is **still relatively niche** but is one of the fastest-growing segments in data center cooling, driven heavily by AI and high-density computing.

### Current Popularity (as of 2025–2026)
- **Overall adoption is low but accelerating**: Liquid cooling (which includes direct-to-chip/cold plate + immersion) is used in roughly **19%** of data centers, up slightly from previous years. Immersion specifically makes up a smaller subset of that.
- Within the broader data center cooling market, **immersion is typically <10%** of total cooling deployments (most liquid cooling today is cold-plate/direct-to-chip).
- Market size for data center immersion cooling is around **USD 1.5–1.7 billion in 2025**, with projections varying widely (USD 8–11 billion by 2035 at CAGRs of ~18–20%).

### Growth Trajectory
Immersion cooling is experiencing very strong growth:
- CAGRs commonly reported between **18–27%** through the early 2030s.
- It is shifting "from a niche ~20% share to dominating" parts of the liquid cooling value chain in the coming 5 years, especially in AI/hyperscale.
- Expected to be particularly strong for ultra-high-density racks (>100 kW), where it outperforms other methods.

### Regional Breakdown
- **North America** leads (often 35–44% share), thanks to hyperscalers and AI investments.
- **Asia-Pacific** is the fastest-growing region (China, India, Japan, etc.).
- Europe is also active, driven by sustainability regulations.

### Who’s Using It?
- Primarily **hyperscalers**, AI training clusters, HPC, and cryptocurrency mining.
- Major players/vendors: Submer, Green Revolution Cooling (GRC), LiquidStack, Asperitas, Fujitsu, Vertiv, Bitfury.
- Some hyperscalers (e.g., Google has significant liquid cooling overall) are deploying it, but full immersion is more selective than broader liquid cooling.

### Why It’s Growing (But Not Dominant Yet)
**Advantages**:
- Excellent for very high densities (100 kW+ racks).
- Major energy savings (up to 40–50% or more vs. air cooling; PUE as low as 1.02–1.10).
- Reduced water usage and smaller footprint.

**Barriers**:
- Higher upfront costs and facility redesign needs.
- Maintenance complexity (fluid handling, retrofitting servers).
- Most existing data centers prefer easier options like cold-plate liquid cooling first.
