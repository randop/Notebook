# Air cooling

**Air cooling is by far the most popular cooling method in data centers worldwide.**

### Current Breakdown (2025–2026)
| Cooling Method              | Approximate Share          | Notes |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------|-------|
| **Traditional Air Cooling** | **70–80%+** of global capacity | Still dominant, especially in existing and smaller/mid-sized data centers. |
| **Liquid Cooling (all types)** | **19–25%** | Includes direct-to-chip / cold plate and some immersion. Growing fast. |
| **Immersion / Dielectric Cooling** | **<10%** (often 5% or less) | Niche but rapidly expanding for AI/high-density. |
| **Hybrid (Air + Liquid)**   | Increasing                  | Common transition approach. |

### Key Insights
- **Air cooling remains king** for the majority of data centers because it’s mature, cheaper to deploy in existing facilities, and sufficient for lower-density racks (typically under 20–30 kW per rack).
- Liquid cooling adoption sits around **19–22%** of data centers having implemented it in some form, but this is heavily concentrated in **hyperscale and AI facilities**.
- Within liquid cooling, **cold plate / direct-to-chip** is currently the most widely used subtype (often >50% of liquid cooling deployments) because it’s easier to retrofit than full immersion.

### Why Air Cooling Dominates
- Lower upfront costs
- Easier maintenance and retrofitting
- Proven reliability over decades
- Adequate for most traditional workloads

However, with the explosion of AI (which pushes racks to 100 kW+), air cooling is increasingly supplemented or replaced in new high-density builds.

**Bottom line**:  
If you look at the **entire world’s data center fleet** today, **air cooling** is overwhelmingly the most popular. Liquid cooling (especially cold plate) is the fastest-growing challenger, while dielectric/immersion cooling is still a high-growth niche.
