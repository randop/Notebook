# Philippines SaaS

## Landscape:

---

### Market Size & Structure

The Philippines Cloud & SaaS enterprise market is valued at approximately USD 3.2 billion. The total combined revenue of homegrown SaaS companies headquartered in the country is far smaller — 50 Philippine-based SaaS companies collectively generate $492M in revenue, employing around 4,900 people, and have raised $12.9M in capital serving 155,100 customers combined.

The market is split between:

**Foreign SaaS consumption** — Philippine enterprises buying global platforms (Salesforce, Microsoft 365, SAP, Zoom, Google Workspace). This is the dominant share of the $3.2B figure.

**Homegrown SaaS production** — Philippine-founded companies building vertical software primarily for domestic and regional markets. This tier is nascent but growing.

---

### Homegrown SaaS Companies: Tier Breakdown

**Top revenue earners (2024–2025):**

- Outsourced, an ISO-certified offshore staffing company, reached $114.8M in revenue in 2025 — the largest by revenue among Philippine-headquartered SaaS-adjacent companies.
- Kumu, a live-streaming social platform launched in 2018, reached $33.7M in revenue in 2025. Digital Booky reached $33.3M in 2024, up from $15.9M in 2023. PayMongo reached $25.5M in 2024.
- Sprout Solutions reached $18.3M in 2024, up from $14.5M in 2023. Kalibrr, a talent-matching platform, reached $19.3M in 2024.

**Sprout Solutions** is the most prominent pure B2B SaaS product company. Established in 2015, it is the largest homegrown B2B SaaS company in the Philippines by annual recurring revenue, focused on HR and business solutions. As of April 2025, it is used by over 240,000 companies in the Philippines and Thailand, and is pursuing a Series C raise of $20M to fund expansion and acquisitions.

**PayMongo** is the dominant payment infrastructure layer. It has partnerships with three major banks — BDO, Metrobank, and Landbank — and holds PCI-DSS 4.0 and SOC 2 Type 2 compliance, fully regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas.

---

### Vertical Composition

Philippine homegrown SaaS clusters around a narrow set of verticals:

**HR & Payroll** — Sprout Solutions dominates. The segment exists because Philippine labor compliance (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, BIR) is complex enough to make manual management painful for mid-market companies.

**Payments/Fintech infrastructure** — PayMongo. The driver here is the structural gap in traditional banking reach. Digital payments surged from 1% of retail payments in 2013 to 52.8% in 2023, creating runway for payment SaaS layers.

**Food & Beverage** — Digital Booky targets F&B digital transactions. The segment is large because the Philippines has an extremely dense restaurant and food-service sector, particularly in Metro Manila.

**Talent/Recruitment** — Kalibrr. This exists on top of a large BPO ecosystem that constantly cycles through high-volume hiring.

**Social/Creator platforms** — Kumu. Not enterprise SaaS in the traditional sense but operates on a platform-subscription model.

---

### Demand-Side Structure: Who Is Buying

**Large enterprises and BPOs** are the most immediate addressable market for B2B SaaS — they have IT budgets, English-language operations, and defined software procurement processes.

**SMEs are the structural problem.** Filipino SMEs continue to lag their regional counterparts in digital adoption. Of those who sought external finance in 2024, only 22% found it easy to secure, and 40% cited rising costs as a major barrier to growth. SMEs account for 63% of the Philippines' total employment and contribute 36% to gross value added, yet many operate at low productivity levels and face challenges scaling due to limited access to finance.

This creates a paradox for SaaS builders: the numerically largest customer segment (SMEs) has the weakest SaaS buying capacity.

---

### Infrastructure Conditions

Mobile internet penetration, measured by unique subscriptions to mobile internet, increased from 45.5% in 2019 to 56.3% in 2022, before falling to 54.1% in 2024. Fixed broadband subscriptions remain low at 7.9 per 100 people as of 2022.

The fixed broadband figure is critical for enterprise SaaS — cloud-based ERP, HRIS, and collaboration tools require reliable fixed connections, which remain underpenetrated outside Metro Manila, Cebu, and Davao. The number of installed telecommunications towers increased from 17,850 in 2020 to 35,043 in 2023, but coverage quality varies sharply between urban and rural contexts.

The government is addressing this: the Philippines Digital Infrastructure Project, approved in October 2024, is investing in the national fiber optic backbone and connectivity to rural schools and health facilities.

---

### Funding & Capital Environment

The Philippine startup ecosystem ranks #64 globally and #6 in Southeast Asia. The ecosystem's total value reached $6.4 billion in 2024, up from $3.5 billion in 2023. Two startups have achieved unicorn status, collectively raising more than $2.5B.

Those unicorns are Mynt (GCash's parent) and Voyager Innovations (Maya) — both are fintech, not pure SaaS. The homegrown SaaS sector's aggregate capital raised is thin: the top 50 Philippine SaaS companies have raised only $12.9M in capital combined. This reflects the absence of deep local venture capital specifically allocated to enterprise software, and the relative difficulty of attracting international SaaS-focused VCs compared to fintech or consumer platforms.

---

### Competitive Dynamics

**Global SaaS vendors dominating enterprise** — Microsoft, Google, SAP, and Salesforce own the large enterprise layer. Philippine SaaS companies do not compete with them directly; they typically occupy gaps those platforms don't address for local compliance, localized workflows, or price-sensitive mid-market.

**BPO ecosystem as both customer and constraint** — The BPO industry (~1.6M workers, ~$32B in revenues) is a consistent enterprise SaaS buyer but also creates a human-labor substitute for software. Companies that could automate via SaaS often instead staff the function offshore.

**Vertical SaaS is where local companies have ground** — Localized HR compliance, Tagalog-language UX, BSP-regulated payments, and BIR-integrated accounting are areas where foreign SaaS either ignores or handles poorly. This is the structural moat for Philippine-built software.

---

### Regulatory Environment

The **Data Privacy Act of 2012** (RA 10173) governs data handling — SaaS vendors storing Philippine personal data must comply with National Privacy Commission requirements. This adds compliance overhead for foreign SaaS entering the market and is a mild differentiator for locally compliant homegrown tools.

The **BSP** directly regulates payment SaaS via e-money issuer and payment system operator licensing. PayMongo's regulatory compliance is not optional — it is a prerequisite for operating in that vertical.

The **DTI's MSME Digitalization Agenda 2023–2028** aims to push SaaS adoption down-market toward micro and small enterprises, but uptake remains uneven given the financing constraints at the SME level.

---

### Organizational Signal: SaaScon PH

SaaScon PH, now in its third edition (April 2025), drew over 1,300 attendees in 2024 and brings together founders, investors, and decision-makers from across the Philippine and Southeast Asian tech ecosystem. Its existence and scale indicates that the Philippine SaaS builder community is organized enough to run an annual industry conference but is not yet large enough for multiple competing industry events. The agenda has shifted in 2025 toward AI integration — reflecting the same pressure facing the global SaaS market at the application layer.
