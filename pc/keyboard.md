# Mechanical Keyboard Switches

## Classification

### Feel

**Linear** — Uniform resistance from top to bottom of the keystroke. No bump, no click. Favored in gaming for uninterrupted travel.

**Tactile** — A bump at or near the actuation point, felt but not heard as a click. The bump is produced by the stem's leg riding over the leaf contact.

**Clicky** — Tactile bump paired with an audible click, produced by a secondary component (click bar, click jacket, or click leaf) separate from the leaf contact.

**Silent Linear / Silent Tactile** — Standard linear or tactile mechanisms fitted with rubber or silicone pads at the top and bottom of stem travel to muffle bottom-out and top-out noise.

### Actuation Mechanism

**Mechanical leaf-contact** — Stem presses a metal leaf against a second contact to close the circuit. Used by the vast majority of switches below.

**Optical** — Stem interrupts or permits an infrared light beam inside the housing; no metal contacts, reducing debounce delay and contact wear.

**Magnetic (Hall Effect)** — A magnet embedded in the stem moves past a Hall sensor on the PCB. Actuation point is software-defined rather than fixed, enabling analog input and adjustable trigger depth.

## Cherry MX Series

Cherry, founded in Illinois in 1953 and manufacturing keyboards since 1973, introduced the MX switch line in 1983. Every Cherry MX switch consists of a top housing, a colored stem, a coil spring, two gold-plated leaf contacts, and a bottom housing. The cross-shaped stem, four-corner housing footprint, 14×14 mm plate cutout, and gold crosspoint contact system originated with Cherry and became the industry standard once the original patents expired.

## Analog / Hall Effect Ecosystem

Wooting introduced the first widely adopted Hall Effect switch, the Lekker, in 2019, followed by SteelSeries with the OmniPoint switch the same year and Corsair in 2023. Kailh and Gateron later released their own magnetic lines, the Source Series and the Double-Rail Magnetic switches respectively.

| Analog Optical | Razer | Linear (optical/magnetic hybrid) | Adjustable actuation via light-beam and magnetic sensing |

## Housing and Stem Materials

- **Polycarbonate (PC)** — Common top-housing material; transmits light well, giving a brighter, higher-pitched acoustic signature.
- **Nylon (PA66)** — Common bottom-housing material; deadens sound for a deeper tone.
- **POM (Polyoxymethylene)** — Common stem material; low friction, self-lubricating, contributes to smoothness.
- **UHMWPE** — Occasionally used in premium stems for reduced friction versus standard POM.

## Spring Types

- **Single-stage spring** — Constant coil diameter; force increases linearly with travel.
- **Progressive spring** — Coil diameter changes along its length, increasing resistance disproportionately near bottom-out.
- **Long-pole spring** — Extended spring seated deeper in the stem, associated with a fuller, rounder bottom-out feel.

## Glossary

- **Actuation point** — Distance the stem travels before the switch registers a keypress.
- **Bottom-out** — Point at which the stem hits the bottom housing at full travel.
- **Pre-travel** — Distance from rest to the actuation point.
- **Debounce** — Firmware delay added to prevent a single physical press from registering as multiple keystrokes.
- **Rapid Trigger** — Magnetic-switch feature that re-arms actuation the instant the key begins moving upward, rather than waiting for full release.
- **Stem wobble** — Lateral play in the stem within the housing, generally considered undesirable.
