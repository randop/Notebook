# Soldering Iron

### Heat up duration
**It typically takes 30 seconds to 5 minutes for a soldering iron to heat up properly, depending on the type, wattage, and model.**

### Quick Breakdown by Type
- **Basic / standard electric soldering irons** (e.g., 25–60W): Usually **1–5 minutes** (often 3–5 minutes recommended). Lower-wattage ones (20–40W) take longer.
- **Adjustable temperature irons or soldering stations**: **15–60 seconds** (many modern ones reach 300–350°C in 20–40 seconds).
- **Soldering guns** (high wattage, trigger-style): Around **20–60 seconds**.
- **Gas-powered or high-end quick-heat models**: As fast as **10–20 seconds**.

Higher wattage generally means faster heat-up. Temperature-controlled stations are much quicker and more consistent than cheap fixed-temperature irons.

### How to Tell When It's Ready
Don't rely only on time — **test it**:
- Touch solder wire to the tip. It should melt **quickly and flow smoothly**, forming a shiny coating (tinning) on the tip.
- If it takes several seconds to melt or just beads up, wait longer.

Early smoke or a hot feel doesn't always mean it's ready — the tip may not be at full soldering temperature yet (typically 300–400°C / 570–750°F).

### Tips for Best Results
- **First use**: New irons may smoke a bit (burning off protective coating) — wipe the tip clean and tin it once hot.
- **Environment**: Cold or windy conditions slow heating; shield the iron if needed.
- **Don't leave it on too long unused** (especially non-temperature-controlled ones) to avoid tip oxidation.
- Always tin the tip after heating and clean it on a damp sponge before soldering.
