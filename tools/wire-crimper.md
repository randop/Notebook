## HSC8 6-6A

The **HSC8 6-4A** and **HSC8 6-6A** (commonly branded as IWISS, Preciva, or similar) are popular self-adjusting ratchet crimping tools designed for **wire end ferrules** (bootlace sleeves) on stranded wires, typically in the range of **0.25–6 mm² (AWG 23–10)**.

### Key Similarities
- Both are self-adjusting (automatically adapt to ferrule size).
- Both feature a ratcheting mechanism for consistent, high-quality crimps with force-saving design.
- Both support single and twin ferrules in the same wire gauge range.
- Both are compact, ergonomic hand tools for electrical wiring, panel building, and terminal blocks.

### Main Differences
The primary difference is the **crimp profile** (shape of the crimp produced):

| Feature              | HSC8 6-4A                          | HSC8 6-6A                          |
|----------------------|------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Crimp Shape**     | Square (quadrilateral, 4-sided)   | Hexagonal (6-sided)                |
| **Jaw Design**      | Four serrated crimp surfaces      | Six-sided sawtooth jaws           |
| **Best For**        | General use; excellent contact in spring-loaded or screw terminals (e.g., DIN rail blocks) | Better fit in round terminal openings; closer to circular shape for space-saving or round sockets |
| **Crimp Quality Perception** | Strong and reliable; fully encapsulates strands | Slightly more uniform pressure distribution; often preferred for finer stranded wires or aesthetics |
| **Common Feedback** | Easier on larger 6 mm² ferrules for some users | Considered an "upgrade" by many; minor visible difference in crimp strength, but hex fits round terminals better |

### Which to Choose?
- Choose the **6-4A** (square crimp) if you prioritize broad compatibility with standard terminal blocks or if it's cheaper/more available.
- Choose the **6-6A** (hex crimp) if you work with round terminals, want a more "professional" or tighter fit, or prefer the hexagonal profile (it's often recommended as slightly superior for most applications).

Both produce excellent, gas-tight crimps when used correctly, and the real-world difference is subtle for most DIY or professional uses. Prices are similar, and many users own both for versatility.
