# Hakata noodles

**Hakata noodles** refer to the signature thin, straight ramen noodles used in **Hakata ramen** (also known as tonkotsu ramen), a famous style from the Hakata district of Fukuoka, Japan.

### Key Characteristics
- **Shape and Size**: Very thin (typically 1.1–1.3 mm diameter), straight, and uniform. They cook quickly and have a firm, chewy bite.
- **Texture**: Firm/al dente (often ordered in degrees like "hard" or *barikata*). They absorb the rich broth well without getting soggy too fast, and many shops offer *kaedama* (free extra noodle refills).
- **Dough**: Made with high-protein flour, low water content, and sometimes additives like gluten or egg whites for extra firmness and a clean white color.

### The Full Dish: Hakata Ramen
Hakata ramen pairs these noodles with a milky-white **tonkotsu** (pork bone) broth, simmered for many hours (often 12+) from pork bones, heads, and trotters until creamy and deeply flavorful. Typical toppings include:
- Chashu (braised pork)
- Green onions
- Pickled ginger (*beni shoga*)
- Wood ear mushrooms
- Sesame seeds
- Soft-boiled or seasoned egg

It originated in the Fukuoka area (Kurume/Hakata) as affordable street food and became one of Japan's most iconic ramen styles, popularized by chains like Ichiran and Ippudo.

### Why the Noodles Matter
In Hakata style, the noodles are as important as the broth. Their thinness and firmness balance the heavy, rich tonkotsu soup perfectly. Regional differences in water and climate influence noodle styles across Japan (e.g., thicker/wavier in colder areas).
