# WiFi

## **AC900 vs AC1200** 
The theoretical maximum combined WiFi speeds of dual-band wireless routers using the 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) standard. The number after "AC" is the sum of the max speeds across both frequency bands (2.4 GHz + 5 GHz).

### Key Differences at a Glance

| Feature                  | AC900                              | AC1200                                      | Winner       |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|--------------|
| **Total Theoretical Speed** | ~900 Mbps                         | ~1200 Mbps                                  | AC1200      |
| **Typical Breakdown**    | 2.4 GHz: ~300 Mbps<br>5 GHz: ~600–867 Mbps | 2.4 GHz: ~300 Mbps<br>5 GHz: ~867 Mbps      | AC1200      |
| **Real-World Speed**     | Often 150–400 Mbps (depending on conditions) | Often 200–500+ Mbps                         | AC1200      |
| **Best For**             | Basic use (browsing, light streaming, small homes, ≤100 Mbps internet) | Moderate use (HD/4K streaming, multiple devices, gaming, 100–500 Mbps internet) | AC1200      |
| **Price (historical)**   | Usually cheaper                   | Slightly more expensive                     | AC900 (budget) |
| **Availability Today**   | Older/less common model           | Very common entry-level dual-band option    | AC1200      |

### Quick Summary — Which One Should You Choose?
- **Go for AC1200** if you're buying today — it's faster on the 5 GHz band (better for modern devices), handles more devices and higher internet speeds better, and is the standard entry-level dual-band router from brands like TP-Link, Netgear, etc.
- **AC900** is an older/slower variant (e.g., some TP-Link Archer C900 or Belkin/Linksys models) and is only worth considering if you find a great deal on one and your needs are very basic.

**Important note**: These are **theoretical** maximums — real speeds are usually 40–60% lower due to walls, interference, device quality, and distance. For most homes in 2025, an AC1200 (or newer Wi-Fi 6 AX routers) is plenty unless you have gigabit+ internet or dozens of heavy devices.
