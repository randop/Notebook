# Dye

A **color dye fixing agent** (also called dye fixative, fixing agent, or color fixative) is a chemical used in textile dyeing to **lock** dyes onto fabrics, preventing color bleeding, fading, or washing out over time. It improves **color fastness** (resistance to washing, perspiration, rubbing, etc.) and keeps colors vibrant longer.

There are two main contexts for these agents:

1. **During the dyeing process** (especially for reactive dyes on cotton, linen, etc.):
   - A substance like **soda ash** (sodium carbonate, often called "dye fixer") raises the pH and triggers the chemical reaction that permanently bonds reactive dyes to the fiber molecules.
   - Without it, many fiber-reactive dyes won't fix properly and will wash out easily.

2. **After dyeing** (post-treatment or finishing step):
   - These are applied to already-dyed fabrics to bond with any unfixed dye molecules left on the surface.
   - They form stronger links (often via ionic bonding, cross-linking, or film-forming) between the dye and fiber, reducing bleeding in future washes.
   - Common for direct dyes, reactive dyes, acid dyes, or all-purpose dyes on natural fibers like cotton, rayon, silk, wool.

Popular consumer products include:
- **Rit ColorStay Dye Fixative** — A liquid used after dyeing (e.g., with Rit dyes) to reduce fading and bleeding; works on cotton, linen, silk, wool, etc.
- **Jacquard Reactive Dye Fixative** — Enhances washfastness and prevents backstaining.
- **Retayne** — A classic for cotton, linen, and rayon to improve color retention.

In industrial textile processing, modern dye fixing agents are often formaldehyde-free cationic or cross-linking types for better environmental safety and performance.

If you're hand-dyeing at home (e.g., tie-dye or garment dyeing), using a fixative after rinsing is highly recommended for longer-lasting results. For commercial fabrics, these agents are standard in finishing to meet wash-fastness standards.
