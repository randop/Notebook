# MMA (Mixed Martial Arts)

**Mixed Martial Arts (MMA)** is a full-contact combat sport that permits competitors to use techniques from multiple martial arts disciplines within a single bout. It integrates striking (punches, kicks, knees, elbows) and grappling (throws, takedowns, joint locks, chokes, ground-and-pound) elements, allowing fighters to transition between standing and ground fighting phases.

### Core Elements of MMA
- **Ruleset Foundations**: Most professional MMA events follow the **Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts**, established in 2000 by the New Jersey State Athletic Control Board and later adopted by the Association of Boxing Commissions. These rules define legal and illegal techniques, weight classes, round structure, and judging criteria.
- **Weight Classes**: Fighters compete in divisions typically ranging from Strawweight (women: 115 lb / 52 kg) up to Heavyweight (265 lb / 120 kg) or Super Heavyweight in some promotions. Weight cuts and hydration checks are standard.
- **Fight Format**: Bouts usually consist of 3 or 5 rounds (5 minutes each for non-title/championship fights; 5 rounds for titles). A fight can end by:
  - **Knockout (KO)** or **Technical Knockout (TKO)**: Opponent is unable to continue due to strikes or referee stoppage.
  - **Submission**: Fighter taps out or verbally submits due to a hold/choke.
  - **Decision**: Judges score the fight after all rounds (unanimous, split, or majority).
  - **Disqualification** or **No Contest**.
- **Fighting Areas**: Primarily an octagonal or circular cage (fenced ring) to contain action and prevent falls. Gloves are typically 4 oz (small, open-fingered for grappling).
- **Attire**: Shorts (men), shorts and sports bra (women); no shoes. Mouthguards and groin protectors are mandatory.
- **Officials**: One referee inside the cage for stoppages and rule enforcement; three judges at ringside for scoring; a ringside physician.
- **Scoring Criteria** (10-point must system, similar to boxing): Effective striking, grappling/control, aggression, and octagon control. Judges evaluate damage, dominance, and duration of control.
- **Illegal Moves**: Headbutts, eye gouges, biting, hair pulling, groin strikes, small-joint manipulation, spine or back-of-head strikes, etc. (with variations by promotion/athletic commission).
- **Origins and Evolution**: MMA draws from ancient pankration (Greek Olympics), early 20th-century mixed-style challenges, and 1990s "no-holds-barred" events. Modern MMA standardized in the 1990s–2000s through organizations codifying rules to improve safety and legitimacy. Key influences include Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (ground game), Muay Thai/Kickboxing (striking), wrestling (takedowns), and Judo/Sambo.

MMA events are sanctioned by state athletic commissions in the US and equivalent bodies elsewhere. Fighter safety protocols include medical suspensions, pre/post-fight exams, and anti-doping testing (e.g., USADA or similar).

**UFC (Ultimate Fighting Championship)** is a professional MMA promotion company, not the sport itself. It organizes, markets, and broadcasts events featuring MMA bouts under the Unified Rules (with minor variations).

### Structure of UFC
- **Ownership and Scale**: Founded in 1993 in the US. It became the dominant global MMA promoter after acquisitions and regulatory acceptance. Events feature ranked fighters, title fights, and prospects.
- **Event Format**:
  - **Main Card**: High-profile bouts, often including title fights.
  - **Prelims and Early Prelims**: Lower-ranked or developmental matchups.
  - **Fight Nights** and numbered events (e.g., UFC 300).
- **Divisions and Titles**: One undisputed champion per weight class (men and women). Interim titles are sometimes used. Rankings are maintained internally and influence matchmaking.
- **Contract System**: Fighters are under exclusive contracts. Pay includes show money, win bonuses, performance bonuses (e.g., Fight of the Night, Performance of the Night), and PPV revenue shares for select stars. There is also a "roster" with developmental pathways (e.g., Dana White's Contender Series).
- **Broadcast and Business**: Pay-per-view model (main events), with prelims on cable/digital platforms. International events adapt to local regulations.
- **Key Features**: Octagon branding, strict weigh-ins (with same-day checks in some cases), fighter ranking system, and medical/safety standards enforced via athletic commissions. The promotion emphasizes entertainment alongside competition, with walkouts, entrances, and highlight-reel finishes.

UFC operates under the oversight of local athletic commissions for each event location. It does not create the sport's rules but adheres to and influences them through its scale.

This covers the factual structure, rules, and operational distinctions between MMA as the sport and UFC as its leading promoter.
